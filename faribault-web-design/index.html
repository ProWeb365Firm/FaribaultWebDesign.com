Common Questions

Everything you need to know before hiring a web designer

Answered completely and honestly, no sales spin.

Get Your Free Call
1. How do you research a specific industry, like manufacturing or agriculture, before designing a website for it?

The discovery and architecture phase involves audience research and competitive analysis relative to the client’s sector, including how AI tools categorize the industry, before design work begins. This means a machine shop and a farm equipment dealer would receive different site structures and messaging even if their budgets and page counts were identical, because their customers search and evaluate businesses differently.

2. What information should I bring to the free strategy call to make it most useful?

Having a clear sense of your typical customer, your top two or three competitors, and specific business goals (more calls, more quote requests, more online orders) structures the 30-minute strategy call to move directly into useful recommendations rather than general questions. Existing analytics data or a list of current website frustrations, if you have a running site, also speeds up the discovery process.

3. Can a website really help a manufacturing or agricultural business that relies mostly on referrals and word-of-mouth?

A professional website acts as a tiebreaker when word-of-mouth reputation is equal between two providers, often serving as the only first impression for customers coming from outside the immediate Faribault area. Even referral-heavy businesses benefit from a site that confirms credibility once a referred customer looks the business up before calling.

4. What's included in the "SEO & Performance Pass" before launch, specifically?

That stage covers on-page SEO elements, schema markup implementation, a Core Web Vitals performance check, and submission of the site’s sitemap to Google, all completed before the site goes live instead of a follow-up task. Bundling this into the pre-launch process, rather than treating it as an add-on, is why every site ships with full schema markup at no extra cost.

5. How does the 30-day post-launch monitoring period work, and what happens if something needs fixing?

The first 30 days after launch involve active monitoring of the new site’s performance and functionality, catching any technical issues or unexpected behavior before impacting customer experience. Since training on content updates also happens at launch, this window gives new site owners a safety net while they get comfortable managing the site themselves.

6. Will my new website be structured to be cited by ChatGPT or Perplexity, not just rank in Google?

Sites are built with structured content, schema markup, and entity data specifically intended to help AI search tools recognize and cite the business when answering local queries, not to satisfy traditional Google ranking factors. This dual focus reflects the shift in how Rice County customers increasingly discover local businesses through AI-generated answers alongside standard search results.

7. What if my business doesn't fit neatly into one of the listed industries, like manufacturing, agriculture, or professional services?

Businesses not fitting the listed industry categories are still invited to reach out directly, since the underlying design and research process adapts to virtually any business type in Rice County rather than being limited to a fixed template per industry. The discovery call is where a business’s specific niche and customer base get mapped out regardless of which broad category it falls closest to.
